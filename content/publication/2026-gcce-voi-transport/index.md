abstract: "Flood monitoring in underground culverts must balance energy and reliability across distinct urgency phases. This paper proposes transport selection driven by the Value of Information (VoI) of the sensed state, rather than by physical-layer link quality, and presents a proof-of-concept on real hardware. A finite-state machine with 0.05 m hysteresis, three-sample debounce, and a state-hold rule maps four water-level states (stable baseline, rising onset, flood peak, recession) to heterogeneous LoRa or Wi-Fi links, while a bidirectional three-failure fallback and a channel-agnostic MQTT sink provide robustness without upper-layer transport awareness. A five-node Raspberry Pi Pico 2W prototype replaying a controlled typhoon-like water-level trace (1800 s, 299 packets) attains the highest packet delivery ratio (PDR = 0.992) among four strategies, and under a relative link-class energy model draws roughly half the per-packet energy of an Always-Wi-Fi baseline."
